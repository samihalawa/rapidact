# What counts as a deepfake under the EU AI Act?

**Any AI-generated or AI-altered audio, image or video that resembles real people, places or events and could pass as authentic. For businesses that includes AI models, AI voiceovers, face swaps and AI-edited campaign photos — not just celebrity scams.**

## The business-relevant definition

Article 3(60) defines deep fakes as AI-generated or manipulated content that "resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events and would falsely appear to a person to be authentic or truthful". Translate that to marketing:

- AI fashion models wearing real products → deepfake territory. Label.
- AI voiceover indistinguishable from a human → label.
- AI background swap in a product photo, product untouched → usually fine, but label if it could mislead.
- A clearly cartoonish AI mascot → not realistic, low risk.

## The labeling duty

Article 50(4): deepfakes must be disclosed **visibly and clearly** when published. [Full guide →](/requirements/deepfake-labeling)
