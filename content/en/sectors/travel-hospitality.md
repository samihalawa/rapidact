# EU AI Act for Travel & hospitality: what you must disclose by 2 August 2026

what hotels, airlines and travel agencies must disclose under Article 50. This is the plain-English playbook — what triggers the law in travel & hospitality, what to fix first, and how to do it without consultants.

## Where Article 50 hits travel & hospitality

| AI you probably use | What the law wants |
|---|---|
| Booking and concierge chatbots | Reservation assistants and AI concierges chatting with guests — disclose at first contact. |
| AI voice booking lines | Voice AI answering calls must identify itself as AI to callers. |
| AI-generated destination content | Synthetic imagery and AI-written travel guides on public pages. |

## The order of operations

1. **Scan one representative public page** — start with the page where visitors interact with AI, then repeat for other public workflows if useful. [Free scan →](/scanner)
2. **Establish the roles** — identify the provider, deployer and exact Article 50 trigger for each system.
3. **Implement the right notice** — use the [free one-script badge](/article-50#install) for a direct-interaction disclosure where it fits.
4. **Treat content separately** — distinguish provider machine-readable marking from deployer duties for deepfakes and public-interest text.
5. **Keep proportionate proof** — record live URLs, wording, owners and dated desktop/mobile checks without tracking visitors.

## Sector reality check

Voice is the channel everyone forgets: if an AI answers your phone line naturally, Article 50(1) applies exactly as it does to web chat.
