# EU AI Act for E-commerce: what you must disclose by 2 August 2026

online stores are the biggest Article 50 target. What your shop must disclose by 2 Aug 2026 and the free fix. This is the plain-English playbook — what triggers the law in e-commerce, what to fix first, and how to do it without consultants.

## Where Article 50 hits e-commerce

| AI you probably use | What the law wants |
|---|---|
| Support and sales chatbots | Tidio, Gorgias, Zendesk and similar widgets answering shoppers automatically — disclosure required at first contact. |
| AI product photos and virtual models | Garments rendered on AI models, AI-generated lifestyle shots — synthetic imagery that must be labeled. |
| AI-written product descriptions and blogs | Bulk AI copy on public pages — label it unless a human editor genuinely reviewed and owns it. |

## The order of operations

1. **Scan everything public** — your site, booking flow, and any client-facing portal. [Free scan →](/scanner)
2. **Establish the roles** — identify the provider, deployer and exact Article 50 trigger for each system.
3. **Implement the right notice** — use the [free one-script badge](/article-50#install) for a direct-interaction disclosure where it fits.
4. **Treat content separately** — distinguish provider machine-readable marking from deployer duties for deepfakes and public-interest text.
5. **Keep proportionate proof** — record live URLs, wording, owners and dated desktop/mobile checks without tracking visitors.

## Sector reality check

Shoppers don't read terms of service. The disclosure has to live where the shopping happens: above the chat, on the image, near the text.
